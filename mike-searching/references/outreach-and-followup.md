# Outreach, replies, and follow-up

Use this reference for creators, media, growers, retailers, distributors, dealers, and strategic partners. Read `flavorwise-product-baseline.md` before using product claims.

## 1. Pre-draft checklist

Confirm the recipient's identity, role, account type, working language, time zone, contact route, current relevant activity, audience/customer, and one verifiable Content Anchor. A Content Anchor can be a specific article, video, podcast, comparison, experiment, product line, customer problem, or business activity. Record the source URL and publication/verification date for each material item.

Do not claim long-term attention, personal experience, product use, purchase intent, or partnership interest without evidence.

Before outreach, confirm that the market and the recipient's relevant business activity are legally and operationally clear. If not, stop at `WAIT FOR EVIDENCE`; never suggest evading regulation.

## 2. Message rules

Every first message should follow this logic:

`Specific Content Anchor → actual problem or value for the recipient → why FlavorWise may be relevant → one small next step`

### Recipient Value First

Start with what the recipient, their audience, readers, growers, or customers may gain. Do not open with `We built`, a company biography, or a generic brand vision.

### Observation over Evaluation

Prefer:

- `I came across...`
- `I noticed...`
- `One detail that caught my attention...`
- `Your comparison included...`
- `You discussed...`

Avoid empty praise such as `I love your work`, `I admire...`, `Great article`, or `Your content is amazing`. An observation must connect immediately to a real question or use case.

### FlavorWise product bridge

Unless the user explicitly changes the product context, a first message normally explains FlavorWise in one or two natural sentences as a `3-in-1 Drying + Curing + Storage` system for roughly `8-10 lbs wet flower capacity`. Keep the bridge proportional to the relationship stage; do not turn it into a product manual.

Use only verified current product claims. Do not promise terpene retention, aroma improvement, flower-quality improvement, mold prevention, guaranteed consistency, drying-speed advantage, laboratory superiority, or guaranteed results. If evidence is not confirmed, write it as a test question: `It may be worth evaluating how it performs in your workflow`, not `it improves` or `it guarantees`.

### One Email One Job

The first message should do one main job only:

- Creator with no verified harvest: see whether the topic fits content testing or whether the recipient can introduce an actual Grower; do not imply the Creator can test the product.
- Grower or Creator with verified harvest: see whether a real harvest test is worth discussing.
- Media: see whether a factual overview or independent test fits its editorial direction.
- B2B/Retailer/Dealer: see whether Product or Procurement should evaluate the category fit.
- Distributor: confirm market and category fit before discussing volume, margin, MOQ, territory, demo, or warranty.

Do not bundle review, affiliate, ambassador, distribution, pricing, discount, territory, meeting, and content rights in the first message.

### CTA

Ask for one low-friction action: send a one-page overview/specs, confirm a harvest window, confirm interest in a test idea, or forward the message to the right Product/Editorial/Cultivation/Procurement contact. Cold outreach should normally not begin by asking for a meeting or call.

### Localization

Choose the language in this order:

`recipient's working language → receiving team's language → local business language → English fallback`

Subject and body use one language. Rewrite for local business habits rather than translating word-for-word. When presenting a non-Chinese draft for review, always include a complete Chinese translation. Actual sending should contain only the target language.

### Signature

For a simple first touch, use:

```text
Mike
FlavorWise
https://flavorwisetech.com/
```

Do not invent a phone number, mailbox, title, discount, shipping commitment, or other signature detail.

## 3. Reusable first-message skeletons

Use these as structural prompts, not copy-and-paste templates. Replace every bracket and keep the Anchor specific.

```text

I noticed [verifiable Content Anchor], especially [specific detail]. It made me think a real [harvest/post-harvest] comparison could be useful for [audience/problem].

FlavorWise makes a 3-in-1 Drying + Curing + Storage cabinet for roughly 8-10 lbs wet flower capacity. Would it be useful if I sent a short overview so you can see whether it fits a future harvest window?

Mike
FlavorWise
https://flavorwisetech.com/
```

Subject: A post-harvest topic for [specific audience/problem]

## 4. Anti-template and humanization QA

Before sending, check:

1. If the name and company are removed, can the message be sent unchanged to dozens of people? If yes, rewrite.
2. If the Content Anchor is removed, does the product bridge remain unchanged? If yes, the connection is weak.
3. Does the first paragraph start with FlavorWise, “we,” or a company biography? Move to Recipient Value First.
4. Does the message ask for more than one main action? Keep one.
5. Does it imply an unverified result, free machine, fee, discount, shipping, affiliate, territory, or exclusivity? Delete or convert to a question.
6. Does it use company size, follower count, or reply enthusiasm as a substitute for evidence? Add evidence or shorten.
7. Does the CTA require more time than the recipient's current interest level? Lower the friction.

## 5. Follow-up operating procedure

### Before follow-up

Read the latest thread and check for a reply, bounce, auto-reply, colleague response, prior commitment, changed role, or changed website. Do not follow up from a stale assumption.

### Classify the state

Use one primary state:

`NO_REPLY`, `INTERESTED`, `PRICING_REQUEST`, `SAMPLE/PILOT`, `PROCUREMENT_REVIEW`, `OBJECTION`, `WAITING_ON_TIMING`, `REDIRECTED`, `REJECTED`, or `DO_NOT_CONTACT`.

The state determines the next action; do not send the same message to every state.

### Timing

As a default, wait roughly 3-5 business days before the first follow-up and 5-10 business days before a second. Adjust for the recipient's time zone, local workweek, auto-reply, stated harvest/procurement window, and urgency. A later nurture touch may be appropriate after several weeks when there is a credible timing trigger.

### Follow-up rules

- Stay in the original thread where possible.
- Add one new useful fact, answer, or relevant link; do not repeat the entire pitch.
- Ask one easy, useful question.
- Do not repeatedly attach the same catalog, repeat a price, or ask “any update?” without a reason.
- If the recipient gives a clear no, respect it and stop.
- After two or three unanswered follow-ups, park the opportunity with a review trigger instead of escalating pressure.

### State-specific next actions

- `NO_REPLY`: one short reminder tied to the original Anchor and one low-friction CTA.
- `INTERESTED`: confirm the next concrete item—overview, test idea, harvest window, or correct contact.
- `PRICING_REQUEST`: answer only with currently authorized terms and label freight, tax, MOQ, payment, warranty, delivery, validity, and other unknowns. Keep the Pipeline at `已回复` until the recipient confirms a market, quantity, timing, or procurement process; do not jump directly to `商务谈判` or `首单`.
- `SAMPLE/PILOT`: confirm fit, timing, test conditions, deliverables, independence, rights, support, and expected value before committing a unit.
- `PROCUREMENT_REVIEW`: provide the requested factual material and ask for the single next gate or owner.
- `OBJECTION`: acknowledge the actual blocker, answer with verified facts, and offer one relevant next step; do not argue.
- `WAITING_ON_TIMING`: record the trigger and review date; do not manufacture urgency.
- `REDIRECTED`: thank the sender and continue only with the named contact after verifying the route.
- `REJECTED` or `DO_NOT_CONTACT`: record the reason if known and stop.

### Pricing and catalog reply skeleton

Use when a B2B recipient asks for price and/or catalog. Replace every bracket with a currently verified value; do not fill an unknown term from an old quote or memory.

```text
Hi [Name],

Thanks for your reply. I have attached/sent the current FlavorWise product overview for your initial review.

For [market/destination] and [quantity], the currently authorized product price is [price + currency + basis]. [MOQ / Incoterm / VAT / freight / payment / warranty / delivery / quote validity] is [confirmed value] or remains [UNKNOWN / to be confirmed].

Could you confirm the market and an approximate quantity so I can verify the applicable commercial terms?

Mike
FlavorWise
https://flavorwisetech.com/
```

The single CTA is confirmation of market and approximate quantity. If any requested commercial term is not current and authorized, say `WAIT FOR EVIDENCE` internally and leave it out or mark it clearly as to be confirmed.

Commercial-term source priority is: latest explicit user authorization → latest formal quote/contract → current official commercial source. A historical snapshot is not current authorization.

### Follow-up skeleton

```text
Hi [Name],

Following up on my note about [specific Anchor/use case]. I thought [one new useful fact] might help with the initial review.

Would [single low-friction action] be the right next step, or should I close this for now?

Mike
```

The final line may be softened or removed for local business norms, but never add a second CTA.

## 6. Reply handling

Classify the actual attitude before drafting: interested, information request, pricing request, sample/pilot, procurement, objection, waiting, redirect, reject, or unclear. Update the stage and forecast only from the new evidence. Reply to what was asked, protect unconfirmed commercial boundaries, and keep the next action small.
