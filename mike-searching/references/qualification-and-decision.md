# Qualification, scoring, and decision

Use this reference for discovery, screening, ranking, pilot decisions, sponsorship, paid placements, and B2B opportunities.

## 1. Research before scoring

For each object, confirm as much as possible from current first-party sources:

- identity, company/account type, role, website and active social accounts;
- recent relevant content, business activity, product lines, services, audience or customer type;
- actual grow, harvest, post-harvest, product-testing, procurement, retail, warehousing, installation, or after-sales ability;
- prior partnerships and the format of those partnerships;
- geography, time zone, working language, legal/market constraints, and practical contact route;
- one specific, verifiable reason FlavorWise could be relevant.

For every material item, record the source URL (or source identifier) and the publication date or last-verified date. If a legal market, recipient business activity, or proposed use cannot be verified, stop at `WAIT FOR EVIDENCE`; do not design a regulatory workaround.

Label every item as one of:

`VERIFIED` — directly confirmed by a reliable current source.

`SELF-REPORTED` — stated by the person or company; useful but not independent proof.

`THIRD-PARTY` — reported by an independent source; check date and reliability.

`INFERENCE` — a bounded judgment derived from evidence.

`UNKNOWN` — not found or not safe to assume.

`STALE-RISK` — previously known but time-sensitive; recheck before action.

Do not infer purchase intent from posting content, audience size, a reply, or a request to “learn more.” Do not infer causation from chronology.

## 2. Classify the object and value track

Choose the narrowest useful archetype:

- Creator / KOL
- Media / Editorial outlet
- Independent Grower
- Commercial Grower / Cultivation Facility
- Distributor
- Retailer / Hydro Store
- Equipment Dealer
- Consultant / Service provider
- Genetics, post-harvest, or other Strategic Partner

Keep value tracks separate:

- Creator: Revenue Track and Authority Track.
- B2B: Direct Revenue, Channel/Distribution, and Strategic value.
- Media: editorial/authority value and attributable commercial value.

## 3. Creator scoring

Score only evidence-backed points. Use this consistent conversion for each weighted factor:

- `1.0 × weight`: current direct evidence meets most of the criterion;
- `0.5 × weight`: partial, indirect, self-reported, or dated evidence;
- `0 × weight`: verified counterevidence or clear absence where absence is itself meaningful;
- `UNKNOWN`: insufficient evidence—leave the points open and show a possible score range.

Do not silently turn `UNKNOWN` into `0`. Report the score, evidence coverage, and confidence together. As a practical priority guide, `80-100` is Priority A when evidence confidence is high, `60-79` is Priority B/selective development, `40-59` is nurture or `WAIT`, and `<40` is usually deprioritize or reject. These bands are decision aids, not substitutes for fit, legality, timing, or EV.

## 5. Bias Shield

Run this for important creators, media, test units, sponsorships, paid placements, and B2B opportunities:

1. **Base rate first:** compare with typical response, pilot, publish, order, and repeat rates for the archetype and channel.
2. **Disconfirming evidence:** record at least three verified facts that could make the opportunity fail or be less valuable. Keep these separate from `Missing Variables`; not finding evidence is not a negative fact. If fewer than three verified disconfirming facts exist, say so and list potential failure variables without inventing evidence.
3. **Shadow review:** ask for an independent second read when the opportunity has strong prestige, a large audience, a famous brand, or internal enthusiasm.
4. **Premortem:** imagine the investment failed; list the most likely operational, commercial, timing, and relationship causes.
5. **Missing-variable scan:** identify the unknowns that could change the decision: real harvest, buyer, audience geography, conversion, terms, fulfillment, content rights, timing, or support burden.
6. **Opportunity cost:** compare the proposed time, machine, fee, freight, and support against the best alternative use of those resources.

Guard against Prestige, Halo, Confirmation, Sunk Cost, Recency, Survivorship, large-follower, large-company, high-view, and reply-quality biases.

## 6. Forecast and expected value

Keep probability forecasts distinct from the decision. Forecast the relevant events separately:

`P(Reply)`, `P(Meaningful Conversation)`, `P(Pilot/Test)`, `P(Publish)`, `P(First Order)`, `P(Sales)`, and `P(Repeat)`.

Use a range when evidence is thin. If there is no relevant base rate or evidence, use a qualitative forecast or state that a percentage is not supportable. Keep two value views separate:

- `Cash EV / Contribution Profit EV`: attributable sales and contribution after machine, fee, freight, support, and other real costs;
- `Strategic/Authority upside`: distribution, credibility, SEO, introductions, and market intelligence. Keep it qualitative unless a defensible proxy exists.

`Cash EV range = probable attributable contribution − machine cost − fee − freight − support time − opportunity cost`

State which terms are confirmed, proposed, or unknown. Do not hide uncertainty behind a single precise number.

## 7. Decision gates

- `APPROVE`: evidence, fit, timing, authorized terms, and EV justify the next internal step. It does not by itself authorize spending, shipping, discounting, signing, or promising.
- `NEGOTIATE`: fit is credible but price, freight, deliverables, rights, volume, payment, support, or timing must be clarified.
- `WAIT`: a material variable is missing or the timing is not ready; specify the verification trigger and review date.
- `REJECT`: poor fit, unacceptable risk, no credible value exchange, or opportunity cost is too high.

Machines, test units, fees, discounts, sponsorships, affiliate arrangements, territory, exclusivity, and shipping are not automatic. A claimed “independent opinion” is not a reason to provide a free machine without a defined collaboration structure.

For commercial terms, use this source priority: latest explicit user authorization → latest formal quote/contract → current official product/commercial source. Historical notes or old snapshots are not current authorization. If a current term cannot be confirmed, mark it `UNKNOWN` and use `WAIT FOR EVIDENCE` rather than guessing.
